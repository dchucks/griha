[Samayiki](https://www.samayiki.com/) is a pioneering Hindi webzine covering science, technology, socio-economic issues, cinema, and politics. Originally launched as **Nirantar** ([nirantar.org](http://nirantar.org)) and rebranded as Samayiki in 2007, it is one of India's earliest and most influential platforms for digital Hindi content and blogging.

Founded by me as part of a broader belief in community-driven and collaborative writing, Samayiki is published by a [collective of Hindi bloggers](https://www.nirantar.org/authors/) whose mission is to create content that is genuinely useful, thought-provoking, and not easily found elsewhere in Hindi — particularly on science, technology, and current affairs.

Samayiki maintains a clear editorial standard: no gossip, no fiction, no astrology, no ideological puff pieces, and no reprinted or syndicated content — distinguishing it from general-purpose Hindi blogging platforms of its era and since.

Over its run, Samayiki has been a rare example of sustained, quality-first Hindi digital publishing, predating many mainstream Hindi news portals and helping establish Hindi as a serious language for online discourse on complex topics.

## Notable Articles

{{< badge style="accent" >}}Technology & Internet{{< /badge >}}
- **[Will Google+ Topple Facebook?](https://www.samayiki.com/2011/07/google-plus/)** (2011) — Early analysis of Google+ as a challenger to Facebook's dominance, one of Samayiki's most-read internet pieces.
- **[RockMelt Browser: The Peak of the Facebook Web?](https://www.samayiki.com/2011/01/rockmelt-browser/)** (2011) — Review of the Facebook-integrated browser that was seen as the future of social browsing.
- **[Microsoft Project Natal](https://www.samayiki.com/2010/01/microsoft-project-natal/)** (2010) — Early Hindi-language coverage of what later became Microsoft Kinect.
- **[Hatsune Miku: Japan's Unique Rock Star](https://www.samayiki.com/2010/11/hatsune-miku-vocaloid/)** (2010) — Feature on the Vocaloid virtual singer phenomenon, years before it became globally known.
- **[Warning: AI Models Are Being Trained on Your Data](https://www.samayiki.com/2024/10/using-your-data-for-ai-training/)** (2024) — On AI companies using personal data for training without explicit consent.

{{< badge style="accent"  >}}Science{{< /badge >}}
- **[Father of Modern Oceanography in India](https://www.samayiki.com/2016/05/oceanography-in-india/)** (2016) — Samayiki's most-commented article, drawing responses from scientists and academics across India.
- **[Galileo: Father of Experimental Physics](https://www.samayiki.com/2009/02/galileo-father-of-applied-physics/)** (2009) — Part of a science series bringing landmark scientific biographies to Hindi readers.
- **[Astronomy Can Foster Development](https://www.samayiki.com/2009/01/astronomy-can-foster-development/)** (2009) — On the role of astronomy in economic and social progress.

{{< badge style="accent"  >}}Politics{{< /badge >}}
- **[Kashmir is Too Small for Independence](https://www.samayiki.com/2010/09/kashmir-too-small-to-be-independent/)** (2010) — A provocative political analysis that generated 14 reader comments, one of the most debated articles on the site.
- **[And Then They Came for Me](https://www.samayiki.com/2009/01/then-they-came-for-me/)** (2009) — Translation of the posthumous final editorial by Lasantha Wickrematunge, the Sri Lankan journalist assassinated for his reporting — a powerful piece on press freedom.
- **[Liberhan Commission Report](https://www.samayiki.com/2009/11/liberhan-commission-report/)** (2009) — Detailed analysis of the 17-year-long Babri Masjid inquiry report.

{{< badge style="accent" >}}Society & Photo Features{{< /badge >}}
- **[Mumbai's AC Local Train — Borivali to Dadar](https://www.samayiki.com/2024/05/borivali-dadar-ac-local-part-1/)** (2024, 2-part series) — Ground-level social reportage on Mumbai's modernising local train system.
- **[Mongolian Nomads: Wandering as a Sacred Right](https://www.samayiki.com/2024/04/mongolian-nomads/)** (2024) — Photo feature on nomadic culture in Mongolia and their constitutional right to roam.
