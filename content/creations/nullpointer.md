[Null Pointer](https://nullpointer.debashish.com/) is my English blog, begun in October 2002, making it one of India's earliest personal technology blogs. The name comes from a classic Java exception (`NullPointerException`), a nod to the blog's original sole focus on Java programming. Over time, as the blog's author evolved from developer to program manager to digital pioneer, so did Null Pointer — expanding into internet culture, Indic blogging, current affairs, politics, and life around us.

The blog's subtitle captures its spirit: like an archer aiming at a dartboard, the intention is always to write pointedly, whether the arrow lands is for the reader to decide.

## A Blog With Many Lives

Null Pointer has lived on multiple platforms across its two-decade history — a journey that itself documents the evolution of the blogging ecosystem in India:

{{< alert type="info" >}}
**Timeline of platforms:**
- **April 2003** — Launched on [Rediff Blogs](http://web.archive.org/web/20050204064735/http://debashish.rediffblogs.com/) — one of India's earliest blog hosting services and the blog's original home
- **July 2003** — Migrated to [JRoller](http://web.archive.org/web/20060106215548/http://jroller.com/page/debashish) — a Java-focused blogging platform where Null Pointer found its technical community and identity
- **2006–present** — Moved to [self-hosted WordPress](http://nullpointer.debashish.com/null-pointer-moves-to-its-own-home) — where Debashish documented the migration process, and the blog has lived ever since.

Before all of this, the author's web presence traces back even further — to the [Geocities](http://www.geocities.ws/debuchakrabarty/) era, including a fan site dedicated to Amitabh Bachchan at `geocities.ws/thisisamitabh`.
{{< /alert >}}

## Beyond Blogging: A Catalogue of Firsts

What makes Null Pointer unusual as a blog is that it is also a window into a wider set of pioneering digital initiatives, many of which are documented here or grew out of conversations on this blog:

{{< card title="DMOZ Editor" image="/images/creations/dmoz.gif" imageAlign="right" imageWidth="300px">}}
Debashish served as an editor at [DMOZ](http://web.archive.org/web/20070807112634/http://www.dmoz.org/profiles/debashish.html), the Open Directory Project, at the time the most authoritative human-curated web directory in the world, powering search results for Google and others. Being a DMOZ editor was a mark of significant credibility in the early internet era. DMOZ snapshot website is available at https://www.odp.org.
{{< /card >}}
<br>
{{< card title="Sarvagya: Hindi Internet Usage Wiki" image="/images/creations/sarvagya.png" imageAlign="left" imageWidth="147px">}}
Debashish was instrumental in creating [Sarvagya](http://web.archive.org/web/20070629113445/http://www.akshargram.com/sarvagya/index.php/Main_page), a wiki established by [Pankaj Narula](https://pnarula.com/) based on MediaWiki OSS, providing primers and advanced tips for using Hindi and other Indic languages (Unicode) on the internet. One of the earliest such community generated resources for vernacular internet users in India.
{{< /card >}}
<br>
{{< card title="Chitthakar: Mailing List for Hindi Bloggers"  image="/images/creations/chitthakar.gif" imageAlign="right" imageWidth="150px">}}
Debashish moderated [Chitthakar](http://groups.google.com/group/Chithakar), a Google Groups mailing list for Hindi bloggers that grew to over 800 subscribers, serving as the primary community hub for the Hindi blogosphere in its early years.
{{< /card >}}
<br>
{{< card title="Global Voices Online: Hindi Lingua Project" >}}
Founded the Hindi Lingua Project of [Global Voices Online](http://www.globalvoicesonline.org/about/), a non-profit global citizens' media project founded at Harvard Law School's Berkman Center for Internet and Society. Debashish continues to contribute to Global Voices to this day.
{{< /card >}}
<br>
{{< card title="Open Source Localisation Contributions" >}}
Contributed Hindi localisations to multiple open-source tools including WordPress, Gregarious, Pebble, IndicJoomla, IJoomla, Scuttle, Snap Shots, Blog Bar, and Splitweet, helping bring these platforms to Hindi-speaking users at a time when Indic language support was far from standard. More details on the [about page](/about).
{{< /card >}}
