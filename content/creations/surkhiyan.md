[Surkhiyan](http://surkhiyan.blogspot.com/) (सुर्खियाँ — meaning *headlines*) and [Daily Songbaad](https://dailysongbaad.blogspot.com/) (বাংলা খবর — meaning *daily news*) were simple but useful news aggregator blogs that pulled the latest headlines from major Hindi and Bengali online news sources respectively, displaying them in a single, easy-to-browse page.

{{< alert type="info" >}}
Both projects were built entirely using Blogger.com's built-in RSS feed widget — no custom code, no backend, no hosting costs. The real effort lay in **identifying, curating, and configuring the right RSS feeds** from across the fragmented landscape of Hindi and Bengali online news.
{{< /alert >}}

## Surkhiyan — सुर्खियाँ (Hindi)

Surkhiyan aggregated headlines from the leading Hindi online news platforms of the time, including:

- **BBC Hindi** — bbc.co.uk/hindi
- **Dainik Bhaskar** — bhaskar.com
- **Navabharat Times** — navbharattimes.indiatimes.com
- **MSN Hindi** — in.msn.com/hi
- **Dainik Jagran** — jagran.com
- **Deutsche Welle Hindi** — dw.com/hi

The goal was simple: a Hindi reader should be able to open one page and scan the top stories from across all major Hindi digital news properties — something that Hindi news portals of the time did not themselves offer in aggregated form.

## Daily Songbaad — বাংলা খবর (Bengali)

A companion project to Surkhiyan, Daily Songbaad applied the same concept to Bengali-language news — aggregating RSS feeds from major Bangla news outlets into a single browsable page for Bengali readers.

## The Effort Behind the Simplicity

{{< alert type="info" >}}
It would be easy to underestimate these projects as trivial. But in 2014, the RSS ecosystem for Indian language news was fragmented and inconsistently maintained — feeds changed URLs, went offline, or were not advertised by publishers at all. Finding reliable, well-structured RSS feeds for each publication, testing them, and keeping the aggregators updated was a genuine curatorial effort.
{{< /alert >}}

Both sites used Blogger.com's native RSS widget feature, which meant:

- **Zero hosting cost** — fully managed by Google's infrastructure
- **Automatic updates** — headlines refreshed as publishers pushed new items to their feeds
- **No maintenance overhead** — once configured, the feeds updated themselves

The simplicity was intentional. These were utility projects — built to be useful, not impressive.
