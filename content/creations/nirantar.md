[Nirantar](http://www.nirantar.org/) (निरंतर, meaning *continuous* or *unceasing*) is a pioneering Hindi blogzine — widely regarded as the **world's first Hindi blogzine** — founded in March 2005 by a group of Hindi bloggers who believed in the power of community and collaborative writing. The name reflects the publication's philosophy: a continuous, unbroken commitment to quality Hindi digital content.

What made Nirantar unique was not merely that it was run by non-professional publishers, but that it gave readers the ability to comment instantly on published articles — making it not just a zine, but a blogzine: a fusion of the blog and the magazine that embodied the spirit of Web 2.0 before that term became ubiquitous.

Nirantar was later reborn as [Samayiki](https://www.samayiki.com/), which continues to publish to this day.

## The Idea: Where Blogging Meets Journalism

In 2005, most Hindi bloggers wrote to share personal opinions. Reportage and investigative writing were nearly absent from the Hindi blogosphere. Nirantar set out to change that — to bring the rigour of journalism to the openness of blogging, and to use the internet's interactivity to make readers co-creators rather than passive consumers.

The publication aimed to reflect the **collective wisdom** of its Hindi-speaking readership — not just the editorial board's views, but a genuine synthesis of community opinion shaped before and after each article's publication. To this end, a *Nirantar Mitra* (Friends of Nirantar) community group was established to involve readers in the editorial process itself.

## Publication History

{{< alert type="info" >}}
**First run (March–August 2005):** Nirantar published six editions before a hiatus due to time constraints on the part of its volunteer editors.

**Revival (August 2006 onwards):** Nirantar returned with a more mature editorial identity — more serious in tone, broader in scope, and with a strengthened core team. Further editions appeared in October 2006, December 2006, February 2007, and July 2008.

Between March 2005 and July 2008 we published 11 editions. Nirantar was later merged to Samayiki which started publishing in a pure blog format rather than a magazine format.
{{< /alert >}}

In its revived form, Nirantar aimed to become **India's first true community magazine** — one where reader participation was visible not just in comments, but in the shaping of each story. Every article aspired to be a distillation of the internet's Hindi-reading community's collective intelligence.

## The Editorial Vision

Each edition of Nirantar aimed to:

- Cover **social, economic, and political dimensions** of a globalising India
- Speak plainly and honestly — not in bookish language, but in the voice of its readers and editors
- Use Hindi that Hindi-speakers would appreciate, without alienating newcomers to the language
- Strike a balance between traditional and modern communication styles

Topics covered ranged from technology, science, and the internet, to literature, cinema, urbanisation, environment, and Kashmir — reflecting the breadth of the Hindi blogosphere it sought to represent.

## The Editorial Team

One of Nirantar's defining characteristics was its distributed, ideologically diverse team drawn from across the world. Editors and contributors held varying viewpoints, yet collaborated on a single publication — a rare feat for any community-run platform.

## Logo & Visual Identity

![Nirantar Logo](/images/creations/nirantar_logo.jpg)
Nirantar's logo was designed pro bono by Gurgaon-based graphic designer and photographer [Bhavna Bahri](https://www.linkedin.com/in/bhavnabahri/). The logo depicts the **chariot wheel (rathchakra)** inspired by the Sun Temple at Konark, a symbol of perpetual motion and continuity, directly embodying the meaning of the name *Nirantar*.

Bhavna incorporated a **glass effect** on the wheel in keeping with the Web 2.0 aesthetic of the time, and used vibrant yellows and oranges to convey energy and vitality. The design succeeded in making Nirantar's Indian identity visually unmistakable.

## Notable Experiments

### Buno Kahani — Collaborative Storytelling

Before Nirantar's revival, Debashish ran [Buno Kahani](http://bunokahani.blogspot.com/) (*Weave a Story*) — a multi-author collaborative fiction experiment in the Hindi blogosphere. One blogger would begin a story; another would continue it, with no prior discussion of plot or length. The resulting suspense and unpredictability made it both entertaining and genuinely challenging for its contributors.

### Laal Pari — The World's First Interactive Hindi Story

In its revived form, Nirantar introduced what is believed to be the **world's first interactive Hindi story** — a new column where popular blogger [Pratyaksha](http://pratyaksha.blogspot.com/) wrote [Laal Pari](http://www.nirantar.org/tag/interactive-story) (*The Red Fairy*). At the end of each episode, Pratyaksha asked readers to vote on which direction the story should take next. The majority vote determined the plot of the following episode — making readers genuine co-authors of the narrative.
