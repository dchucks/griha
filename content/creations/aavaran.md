[Aavaran](https://aavaran.blogspot.com/) (आवरण, meaning *cover* or *wrapping*) was a repository of ready-to-use Hindi templates for Google's Blogger platform, created in 2005 at a time when Blogger had no native internationalisation support and Hindi bloggers had to make do with English-language default themes.

The name was deliberately chosen — *aavaran* means the cover or outer wrapping of a book or magazine, making it a fitting metaphor for a collection of visual themes for blogs.

{{< alert type="info" >}}
Aavaran was one of the **first resources on the Indian internet** to offer ready-made, Hindi-compatible Blogger templates — lowering the barrier to entry for a new generation of Hindi bloggers who wanted their blogs to look good without knowing CSS or HTML.
{{< /alert >}}

## What Aavaran Offered

Each template in Aavaran was built on top of popular Blogger themes (such as the original China-Red theme for Blogger, adapted from a WordPress 1.5 design by [Rahul Karnik](http://www.rahulkarnik.com/)), and adapted for Hindi use — adjusting typography, layout, and encoding for correct Indic text rendering. Templates were released under open licences and came with clear instructions for installation. The templates were made available as downloadable ZIP files hosted on MyJavaServer.com's free Java hosting — the same server that hosted Chittha Vishwa, the first Hindi blog aggregator.

## Context: Why This Mattered
In 2005, starting a Hindi blog meant wrestling with Unicode encoding, finding an Input Method Editor (IME) that worked, and then discovering that every Blogger theme rendered your Devanagari text in a tiny, unreadable default font. Aavaran solved the last of those problems — giving Hindi bloggers visually polished, community-built themes that worked out of the box.

<br>
Two recurring requests from the community shaped the roadmap for Aavaran's future updates: a bilingual (Hindi + English) description for each template, and adapted versions of popular community templates like the one used by [Chittha Charcha](http://chitthacharcha.blogspot.com/). On the latter, I noted that the licensing terms of the source template were unclear — a reminder that even in the early blogosphere, open-source etiquette mattered.
