[Nuktachini](http://nuktachini.debashish.com/) (नुक्ताचीनी) is one of the earliest Hindi blogs ever published on the internet. Founded in 2004, it holds the distinction of being the **4th Hindi blog ever created** — making it a genuine piece of internet history for India's digital vernacular landscape.

The word *nuktachini* is a Hindi expression meaning criticism or fault-finding — captured in the blog's subtitle *निंदक नीयर राखिये* ("keep your critics near-by"), a line from the poet Kabir. But as early reviewers noted, the blog lives up to its name only in the most playful sense: rather than gossip or negativity, it is filled with thoughtful, useful writing on technology, society, politics, and life online.

The blog continues to be updated to this day, making it one of the longest-running Hindi blogs on the Indian internet.

## Origins: The Very Dawn of Hindi Blogging

{{< alert type="info" >}}
When Nuktachini was founded in 2004, there were only **two Hindi blogs in existence** — Vinay Jain's [Hindi](https://hindi.blogspot.in/) and [Alok Kumar's Nau do Gyarah](https://9211.blogspot.in/). Alok Kumar is also credited with coining the term **चिट्ठा** (*chittha*) — the Hindi word for blog — which became universally adopted across the Hindi blogosphere.

A third blog, belonging to a friend Padmaja, was also created around the same time by Debashish. He then decided to jump on the bandwagon and thus Nuktachini is **officially the 4th Hindi blog ever published**.
{{< /alert >}}

The inspiration for Hindi blogging came during a work stint at [Webdunia.com](https://www.webdunia.com/) — a pioneering organisation in Indic language localisation and one of the earliest Hindi web portals in India. Exposure to the possibilities of Hindi content on the internet, combined with the excitement of the early blogosphere, led directly to the creation of Nuktachini.

![A grabn of the Kadambini magazine page](/images/creations/kadambini-nov2004.png)The blog was noticed from its very early days. It received a mention in **Kadambini**, one of India's oldest and most respected Hindi magazines ([read here](https://nuktachini.debashish.com/37), a mentionable recognition for a personal blog in the pre-social-media era.

## Topics & Sections

Nuktachini covers a wide range of topics, organised into sections that reflect both the personal and the public:

- **ज़िंदगी आनलाईन** (Life Online) — internet culture, technology, and digital life
- **बातें तकनीकी** (Tech Talk) — in-depth technology coverage
- **ब्लॉगिस्म** (Blogism) — reflections on the Hindi blogosphere itself
- **आसपास** (Around Us) — social issues, urban life, and current affairs
- **किस्से कुर्सी के** (Tales from the Chair) — political and bureaucratic commentary
- **रुपहली दुनिया** (The Silver World) — cinema
- **कड़ियाँ** (Links) — curated links from across the web
- **अतिथि का चिट्ठा** (Guest Post) — writing by invited contributors

## Legacy

Nuktachini occupies a unique place in the history of the Indian internet, not merely as an early blog, but as one of a tiny handful of acts of faith that proved Hindi could thrive as a language of serious, thoughtful online discourse. At a time when the prevailing assumption was that India's internet audience was exclusively English-speaking, Nuktachini and its contemporaries demonstrated that Hindi readers were eager, engaged, and hungry for quality content in their own language.

That experiment that begun with just a handful of blogs in 2004 eventually gave rise to the hundreds of thousands of Hindi blogs, YouTube channels, and digital publications that exist today.
