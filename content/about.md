## Digital Beginings

I am proud to be one of the earliest voices on the Indian internet, a period I look back on as genuinely formative for me. My web presence dates to the Geocities era, but it was a stint at [Webdunia.com](https://www.webdunia.com/), one of India's pioneering Indic language localisation organisations, that pulled me deep into Hindi digital content. At the time there were probably only two Hindi blogs in existence. I created the [3rd Hindi blog ever published](/creations/nuktachini/), and helped catalyse a community that eventually produced thousands of Hindi bloggers, publications, and podcasters.

A conviction I've held since the early 2000s is that Indian languages on the internet were not in competition with English, rather they were building something entirely their own. The biggest barrier to Hindi internet was never bandwidth or devices; it was a simple question every newcomer asked: *"How do I type in my language?"* Helping answer that question, through tools, wikis, aggregators, localisation, and community, was the animating purpose behind much of my (and the community's) early digital work.

Created *Chittha Vishwa*, possibly the first-ever aggregator of Hindi blogs, tracking blog statistics, blogger profiles, trivia, and bookmarks for the emerging Hindi blogosphere. It ran on MyJavaServer's free Java hosting until the service shut down. [Read more →](/creations/chitthavishwa/)

<br>
{{< card title="The Indibloggies: India's First Blog Awards" >}}
Founded [The Indibloggies](http://indibloggies.blogspot.com) in 2003, the first and, to my limited knowledge, the only blog awards for Indian blogs across multiple languages, running five editions through 2008. The awards covered English plus Bengali, Hindi, Marathi, Gujarati, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, and Malayalam, with winners chosen through a hybrid of jury shortlisting and public voting. In later editions, sponsorships grew to ₹12 lakh worth of prizes through companies like Synapse.

Some of the winners went on to win big awards and book deals. [Read more →](/creations/indibloggies/)
{{< /card >}}
<br>
{{< card title="Nirantar: World's First Hindi Blogzine" >}}
Founded and published [Nirantar](http://www.nirantar.org) in 2005, possibly the world's first Hindi blogzine, blending the interactivity of blogging with the editorial rigour of a magazine. It later evolved into [Samayiki](https://www.samayiki.com/). [Read more →](/creations/nirantar/)
{{< /card >}}
<br>
{{< card title="Sarvagya: Hindi Internet Usage Wiki" >}}
Was instrumental in creating [Sarvagya](http://web.archive.org/web/20061004213632/http://www.akshargram.com/sarvagya/index.php/Main_page), a wiki providing primers and advanced guides for using Hindi and other Indic languages on the internet via Unicode. One of the earliest such resources for vernacular internet users. Now defunct.
{{< /card >}}
<br>
{{< card title="Global Voices Online — Hindi Lingua Project" >}}
Founded the Hindi Lingua Project of [Global Voices Online](http://www.globalvoicesonline.org/about/), a non-profit global citizens' media project born at Harvard Law School's Berkman Center for Internet and Society. I continue to contribute to Global Voices to this day, whenever I get time.
{{< /card >}}
<br>
{{< card title="Chitthakar: Hindi Bloggers Mailing List" >}}
Founded and moderated [Chitthakar](http://groups.google.com/group/Chithakar), a Google Groups community for Hindi bloggers that grew to over 800 subscribers, serving as the primary hub for the Hindi blogosphere in its formative years. Now inactive.
{{< /card >}}
<br>
{{< card title="DMOZ Editor" >}}
Inspired by fellow blogger [Alok Kumar](http://9211.blogspot.com/), I served as an editor at [DMOZ](http://web.archive.org/web/20070807112634/http://www.dmoz.org/profiles/debashish.html) (the Open Directory Project), at the time the most authoritative human-curated web directory in the world, powering search results for Google and others. DMOZ snapshot website is available at https://www.odp.org.
{{< /card >}}
<br>
{{< card title="Open Source Localisation" >}}
Contributed Hindi localisations to multiple open-source tools including [WordPress](https://web.archive.org/web/20110722095640/https://translations.launchpad.net/~debashish), Gregarious, [Pebble](https://web.archive.org/web/20060301162838/http://www.simongbrown.com/blog/2005/04/25/pebble_goes_international.html), IndicJoomla, IJoomla, [Scuttle](https://web.archive.org/web/20060504220220/https://scuttle.org/wiki/hindi), [Snap Shots](https://web.archive.org/web/20080527181250/http://wiki.snap.com/index.php/Contributors), [Blog Bar](https://web.archive.org/web/20080915144702/http://www.blogbar.org/india.php), and [Splitweet](https://web.archive.org/web/20090217004627/http://hi.splitweet.com/about), helping bring these platforms to Hindi-speaking users at a time when Indic language support was far from standard.
{{< /card >}}
<br>
{{< card title="Bangla Blog Primer" >}}
Created one of the [first tutorials](/creations/banglablogs/) on the web for starting a blog in Bengali using Unicode — helping Bangla speakers enter the blogosphere when no such guidance existed.
{{< /card >}}

Created [Aavaran](/creations/aavaran/) in 2005 — a repository of ready-to-use Hindi Blogger templates at a time when Google's Blogger platform had no native internationalisation support.

## What I Do Today

Cross-functional program leadership, and helping engineers and PMs navigate career transition, whether that's landing a TPM role, building influence without formal authority or maintain the dignity of the career in the AI era without losing confidence.

Beyond the day job, I write for my [newsletters](../#newsletters) on careers and agile leadership, coach professionals 1:1, and host [Podbharati](/creations/podbharati/), a Hindi podcast platform where my latest effort goes to the career podcast *[Sopaan](http://www.podbharati.com/shows/sopaan/)*.
